Your 10 July traps – check these first

  1. Add the preposition – it belongs in the gap: insist on travelling · interested in listening. If it is already printed (was used to (do), looking forward to (enter)), type only the -ing form. [11–12]
  2. suggest / consider (= think about) + -ing: suggested going (to go) · considered leaving (to leave). [6, 10]
  3. Simple past is the default. Past perfect only if something is earlier than another past point: he wasn’t there at 8:59 (hadn’t been). [15–16]
  4. Background in progress → past progressive: we were having a bad time · somebody was feeling around in a drawer, and there at the back was … [16]
  5. Narrator’s NOW inside a past story: whom I will call “Chuck” for the following story · I regret that … I would not be telling the truth now … [14, 17]
  6. Future seen from the past → would (be -ing): we had expected that he would be standing there. [17, 19]
  7. If-clause: read the main clause first. would → if + past (spent) · would have → if + past perfect (had simply reused). [18]
  8. Modal signals: convinced → must · Surely …? → can’t have · …, but → may (have) · ___ better → had · only years later → was to · typical of him → would · no reason to think → needn’t · feared → might · told us we only ___ → had to. [21–24]
  9. Relatives: after a name → comma: , who · object in a defining clause → C · no antecedent at all (what = the thing that) → what; after he / those / anyone → who · many/some/two → of whom · named place → , where (defining: at a time when · the day C). [27–30]
  10. MC: regret to = now / regret -ing = past · condition names go by form · a real past (yesterday) is not modal past · contact clause = 0 pronouns · no commas = only some · no inversion after no doubt / in no time / nonetheless · my own.

2 · Tense & aspect [13–19]

Ask for every gap

① Whose time – the story’s past or the narrator’s now? ② Is it earlier than another past point? → past perfect. ③ In progress / background / temporary behaviour? → progressive. ④ Future seen from the past? → would (be -ing). ⑤ An if-clause? → look at the main clause first.

Present

Simple present: habits · timeless truths · commentaries · historic present (also I hear you have finally changed your mind) · timetable future · after when / as soon as / if with future meaning · state verbs (know, believe, belong, see, regret).

Present progressive: incomplete or temporary · always = annoyance · arrangement (We’re spending our holidays in Canada) · be + adjective = temporary behaviour (You are being silly).

Past

Present perfect: unspecified past (just, already, ever) · up to now (for, since) · result. A definite past time (yesterday, in 1997, at 8:59) → simple past.

Past progressive: frame (at 11 I was reading) · time inclusion (When she was having lunch, the phone rang) · simultaneity · temporary habit.

Past perfect: only for something earlier than a past point of orientation (When the police arrived, the thieves had run away).

Modal functions in the if-clause: should = improbability (If he should call, …) · were to = supposition · will / would = politeness or willingness (If you won’t leave a man in peace = refuse to).

Modal past (hypothetical): wish · if only · as if · it’s (high) time · I’d rather + past; were for every person. A real past with a time marker is not modal past. [15–16]

Reported speech: with a past reporting verb, backshift: present → past · past / present perfect → past perfect · will → would. These modals stay: must (supposition), ought to, used to, had better, could, would, should, might. [19]

had (were having) · called (will call) · looks (looked) · had not been (wasn’t) · stood (would be standing) · had been (was being) · spend (spent) · simply reused (had simply reused) · felt (was feeling) · regreted (regret)

4 · Relative clauses [27–31]

Comma test: can the clause be removed and turned into a new sentence? → non-defining → commas (names, unique things). No commas = defining = it restricts: The travellers who knew = only some of them.

casedefining (no comma)non-defining (commas)
subjectwho / that · which / that – never C, who · , which
objectwho(m) / that / which / C → write C, who(m) · , which
prepositionat whom / which · or C … at, with whom · , for which
genitivewhose, whose · , the history of which
partitive–, many / some / two of whom · of which
  • that never after a comma. C only in defining clauses, never for the subject; also after time nouns (the day C Hale-Bopp …).
  • , which can refer to a whole clause (connective): Jill called me a pig, which I found insulting.
  • what = that which (no antecedent): for what they thought would be a trip.
  • , where / , when only in non-defining clauses after a name or an already identified place/time: in Rancho Santa Fe, where they ran a business. Defining = no comma: at a time when this was a good way … (mock) · the day C Hale-Bopp …
  • whoever / whatever / whichever = no matter who / what; present tense for the future. Cleft: It was he who …
Applewhite who (, who) · compound which (C) · Rancho Santa Fe , (, where) · Heaven’s Gate C (, who) · for who (what) · many who (of whom)

5 · Word order [32–37]

Mid position: before the main verb, after am/is/was, after the first auxiliary (We will probably have arrived). Degree and manner adverbs go after all auxiliaries (will have been completely ruined). With a contracted negative, the adverb comes first (I probably won’t).

End position: manner – place – time (after verbs of motion: place – manner – time).

Inversion after negative or restrictive front adverbials: never · seldom · scarcely · barely · hardly · rarely · only · not only · no sooner · nowhere · under no circumstances · on no account · on no condition · no way · few · little · so · such · neither · nor. Simple verb → do: Rarely did she visit · So severe did the symptoms become. Compound verb or be → no do: Never have I seen · So great was my surprise.

NO inversion: no doubt · in no time · nevertheless · nonetheless · occasionally · without exception · not far from · not long after · even so · with no difficulty at all.

here / there / up / down / off / back + noun → inversion (Here comes the bride!); + pronoun → none (Off we go!).

Adverbs of time: soon · still · recently · lately ≠ frequency: sometimes · often · usually. He spoke even to me = to me of all people.

6 · Determiners [38–40]

the: unique things (the earth) · made definite by a clause or of-phrase · superlatives, ordinals, only · the + adjective = a group of people (the dead) · rivers, seas, mountain chains, island groups, deserts, plural countries (the USA) · instruments and dances.

No the: abstract and uncountable nouns in general (life, sugar) · plural classes (Dogs with long legs …) · names, continents, countries, towns, streets, lakes · body parts and clothes → possessive (his hand; but hit me in the stomach) · school, church, hospital, prison as institutions · man (= mankind) · illnesses (appendicitis; but the flu, a cold).

a / an: countable singular · a class (A cow has horns) · professions (She is a teacher) – but no article for unique positions and titles (appointed Head of Department) · a dozen, a hundred · 60 miles an hour · What a day! · a few / a little = some.

few / little without an article = hardly any: Few people know this; hardly any rain = little rain. But: A few of my friends work abroad.

No a / an: plurals · abstract nouns · uncountable nouns (advice, information, news) · materials · meals (have breakfast; but a good breakfast) · own → my / her own.

7 · MC traps July + mock test

  • Underline NOT / How many … do not – then count the wrong ones.
  • regret to + past time = wrong · regret -ing / having done = right → “how many are not correct?” = 2.
  • If I became President, I would … = improbable (by form – not impossible).
  • Modal past: as if he were · it’s high time you started · I wish I had never come. Not: you came home early yesterday.
  • Contact clause = 0 relative pronouns; the pronoun can be left out only in defining clauses.
  • Present progressive for the future = intention with present arrangement. will = promise. must = strong obligation (speaker’s authority).
  • cannot have escaped = impossibility · could have danced all night = ability, not performed.
  • When you take a look …, you will understand – no will in the time clause.
  • are being silly = temporary behaviour · I hear you have … = historic present.
  • Inversion with compound verbs: no do-paraphrase.
  • Head of Department → no article · own → possessive determiner · a few of my friends = some · hardly any rain = little rain.
  • …, which I found insulting → which refers to the whole preceding clause.
